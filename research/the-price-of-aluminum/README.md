A ten-minute science-and-history video on Facebook. I didn't know any of it, and the gap between "everywhere in the ground" and "precious on the table" was too odd to leave alone.

## Scope

**In:** the history of aluminum from alum to the post-1886 price collapse; the Hall–Héroult and Bayer processes; the energy and cryolite dependencies; the lore that does and doesn't survive checking.

**Out, for now:** any thesis. The draft deliberately stops before the argument. See the frame.

## Claim boundaries

- "Worth more than gold" is stated only as **briefly, by weight, in the early 1850s**, and rests on an `assumed` price.
- The Napoleon III tableware and crown-jewels stories are reported **as lore**, never as events.
- No single cost is given for the Washington Monument tip.
- The draft takes **no side** on Julia Hall's role.
- The price collapse is stated as **more than thirtyfold, 1884–1900**, not as a precise multiple.
