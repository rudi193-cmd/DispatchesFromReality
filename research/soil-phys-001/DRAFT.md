## SECTION 01: THE CONSTANTS AND THE ARENA

Phys. 4 (1901) 553, which ends: "Hieraus und aus (14) ergeben sich die Werte der Naturconstanten" (from this and from (14) follow the values of the natural constants): h = 6,55·10⁻²⁷ erg·sec and k = 1,346·10⁻¹⁶ erg/grad. **[02.03] Choosing a vacuum**

## SECTION 05: WHY NOT ABSOLUTE ZERO

**[05.01] Nature's zeros are never empty**

- SOURCE: A quantum oscillator's lowest energy is E₀ = ½ħω, not zero (quantum mechanics, 1925-26; Planck's 1911 second theory first had it).
- SOURCE: Casimir, Proc. KNAW 51 (1948) 793: two perfectly conducting (metal) plates attract, and "this force may be interpreted as a zero point pressure of electromagnetic waves". CHECKED (the KNAW reprint).
- SOURCE: Nernst (1906) proposes, as his "Fundamentalhypothese", that near absolute zero the maximal work and the energy of a reaction among pure condensed substances coincide, with their temperature derivatives going to zero. CHECKED. The unattainability form came in 1912 (Sitzungsber. Preuss. Akad. Wiss., 134), as "allgemeines Prinzip, aus dem mein Wärmesatz sich ergibt" (a general principle from which my heat theorem follows): "Es ist unmöglich, durch irgendwelche stoffliche Veränderungen von einer selbst beliebig tiefen Temperatur bis zum absoluten Nullpunkt zu gelangen" (it is impossible, by any change of matter, to get from even an arbitrarily low temperature to absolute zero). He opens by noting the general assumption was "dass hier eine Unmöglichkeit vorliegt" (that an impossibility lies here), and calls the result "gewiss merkwürdig" (certainly remarkable), since the vanishing specific heat makes coming close so much easier. CHECKED (OCR of the volume's scan, pages 134 to 140).
- CHANGED (rev 3): Nernst's 1906 paper is a hypothesis about work and energy, not yet a law about entropy.
- SOURCE: Planck, *The Theory of Heat Radiation* (Masius's 1914 translation of the 1913 second edition), §140: "for the zero point of the absolute temperature E becomes, not 0, but Nhν/2", and "That the oscillators are said to perform vibrations even at the temperature zero, the mean energy of which is as large as hν/2 and hence may become quite large for rapid vibrations, may at first sight seem strange." CHECKED.
- READING: That is line 0001's claim in 1913, in its source's own words, and with its own warning that it "may at first sight seem strange".
- SOURCE: Planck (1911), the second theory: the oscillator's mean energy comes out "von dem früheren Werte 11) verschieden um die additive Konstante hν/2" (differing from the earlier value by the additive constant hν/2); at T = 0 it is hν/2. CHECKED (a retyped German text, not a scan of the Verhandlungen).
- SOURCE: Einstein and Stern (1913), the first paper to argue for it from data: "Nach Formel (2) hat der Resonator beim absoluten Nullpunkt die Energie hν/2" (by formula (2) the resonator has, at absolute zero, the energy hν/2), and, in their summary, "Die Euckenschen Resultate über die spezifische Wärme des Wasserstoffs machen die Existenz einer Nullpunktsenergie vom Betrage hν/2 wahrscheinlich" (Eucken's results on the specific heat of hydrogen make the existence of a zero-point energy of size hν/2 probable). But in a footnote, their derivation of Planck's radiation law needs the zero-point energy "gleich hν gesetzt" (set equal to hν), and "Spätere Untersuchungen müssen zeigen" (later work must show) whether the discrepancy goes away. CHECKED (read on the scanned pages, 555 to 560).
- READING: The first argument that nature's zero is full was not sure how full: hν/2 from hydrogen, hν from radiation. The zero point was found before its size was.
- SOURCE: Penrose, Phys. Rev. Lett. 14 (1965) 57: once a trapped surface forms, "deviations from spherical symmetry cannot prevent space-time singularities from arising"; with non-negative local energy and null completeness assumed, "the existence of a trapped surface implies—irrespective of symmetry—that singularities necessarily develop". If physical singularities are not to occur, then inside the collapsing object at least one holds: "(a) Negative local energy occurs. (b) Einstein's equations are violated. (c) The space-time manifold is incomplete. (d) The concept of space-time loses its meaning at very high curvatures—possibly because of quantum phenomena", the four "somewhat interrelated, the distinction being partly one of attitude of mind". CHECKED (read on the page, 58).
- CHANGED (rev 3): revision 2 said "under general conditions, gravitational collapse ends in a singularity; general relativity's predictions fail there". The conditions are a trapped surface and his assumptions (i) to (v); and the failure is not his claim but one of four ways out he lists.
- READING: Penrose's (d) is the nearest his letter comes to this section's reading, that where the theory's zero of volume arrives the concept of space-time itself may lose its meaning. It is one way out of four, not his conclusion.
- READING: Each of these is a place where "zero" is a ground state or a breakdown, never an observed emptiness.

**[05.02] Zero in mathematics**

- SOURCE: von Neumann, Acta Sci. Math. (Szeged) 1 (1923) 199: 0 = ∅, 1 = {∅} = {0}, 2 = {0, 1}, and on.
- SOURCE: Zermelo (1908): the null set enters by Axiom II as "uneigentliche", improper, and the infinite by Axiom VII as 0, {0}, {{0}}, and on (line 0003a); "Die Mengen 0 und {a} besitzen keine Teile" (the sets 0 and {a} have no parts). He credits Axiom VII's content to Dedekind, but says Dedekind's attempted "Beweis" (proof) of it "kann nicht befriedigen, da er von der „Menge alles Denkbaren“ ausgeht" (cannot satisfy, since it starts from the set of everything thinkable): the infinite is taken, not proved. CHECKED.
- SOURCE: Frege, *Die Grundlagen der Arithmetik* (1884), §74: "Weil unter den Begriff »sich selbst ungleich« nichts fällt, erkläre ich: 0 ist die Anzahl, welche dem Begriffe »sich selbst ungleich« zukommt" (since nothing falls under the concept "not identical with itself", I define: 0 is the number belonging to that concept); §77: "1 ist die Anzahl, welche dem Begriffe »gleich 0« zukommt" (1 is the number belonging to the concept "identical with 0"). CHECKED (Project Gutenberg's transcription).
- SOURCE: Dedekind, *Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen?* (1888), §71: the base element of a simply infinite system, "das wir im Folgenden durch das Symbol 1 bezeichnen wollen" (which we will denote by the symbol 1). Beman's 1901 translation: "which we shall denote in what follows by the symbol 1, the base-element". His number series begins at 1, and his preface puts zero among the later extensions of the number concept: "die Schöpfung der Null, der negativen, gebrochenen, irrationalen und complexen Zahlen" (the creation of zero, of the negative, fractional, irrational and complex numbers). CHECKED (OCR of the 1888 scan, and the translation).
- READING: Three founders, three zeros. Frege's counts a concept nothing can fall under, a contradiction put to work; Dedekind starts at 1 and calls zero a later creation; Zermelo admits one as improper. Each puts 1 straight on top of it: Frege's 1 counts the concept "identical with 0", Zermelo's next set is {0}. **[05.03] The synthesis: {(0)}** (all READING)
