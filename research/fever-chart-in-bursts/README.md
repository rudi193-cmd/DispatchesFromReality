## Core question

Does Brinton's 1938 natural history of revolution still describe how a revolution would unfold in a networked society, and if not, what has to change in the model?

---

## Thesis

1. **The stage reading (contested, the author's).** 2008 to 2016 reads as Brinton's old regime cracking (deficits, collapsing household wealth, falling public confidence, blocked young careers and doubled student debt are now sourced; intellectual desertion is shown by example only), 2021 to 2025 as the rule of the moderates, and 2025 onward as an accession testing whether power can move outside the coordinated chain of institutions.
2. **An inversion of dual sovereignty.** In Brinton's four cases the extremists ran an illegal rival government. The 2025 to 2026 court-compliance record describes something else: a faction holding the legal government and contesting parts of it from inside. Brinton's categories do not settle that case.
3. **Thermidor comes from inside.** Robespierre fell to frightened insiders, not to the opposition. The 2026 Republican breaks with the administration are a much milder version of that mechanism, driven by electoral survival.
4. **Continuity under the cycle.** Brinton's inherited institutions are "annoyingly persistent," and the patient is not wholly made over. The Paperclip lineage work found the same: continuity without sameness. Thermidor reads less as a reversal than as the substrate reasserting itself.
5. **The revision.** Modern movements arrive in bursts that spread fast and fade (Tufekci, Chenoweth), but each burst leaves networks, skills, and unresolved grievance behind. The better model is a stock that pools under spiking flows, closer to a self-organized critical sandpile than to a fever with a course.

## Method

- **Read the primary text.** Brinton is quoted from the 1938 first edition on Project Gutenberg (eBook 78065), not from summaries.
- **Tag every claim by provenance.** `measured` (read at first hand), `fitted` (corroborated by a named secondary report), `assumed` (single source or none).
- **State the counter-reading next to the thesis.** The ordinary-politics reading, realignment rather than revolution, is given in full in the draft.
- **Treat the election as a test, not a verdict.** The draft names four tests (acceptance, the check, the insiders, delivery) and will be updated as they resolve.

## Claim boundaries

- A comparative reading against a 1938 framework. Not a prediction, and not a claim that a revolution is underway.
- The stage mapping is the author's thesis and is contested.
- Brinton is quoted from the 1938 edition only; the 1952 and 1965 revisions are not quoted.
- The pooling model and the surveillance hypothesis are proposals. Nothing here measures accumulated capacity.
- The election tally is one snapshot and will not hold.
