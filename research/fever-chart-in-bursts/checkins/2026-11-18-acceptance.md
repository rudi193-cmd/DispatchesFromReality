# 2026-11-18 check-in: acceptance

*First of the four tests set up in [DRAFT.md](../DRAFT.md), section "A data point, not a conclusion". Listed in the [watch list](../willow/watch-list.md).*

## The question

Are the November 3, 2026 midterm results being accepted without a rival claim to their authority? This is Brinton's dual sovereignty test asked directly. Brinton's mark is not a complaint about a result. It is "another and conflicting chain of institutions" that "provides another and conflicting set of decisions." So the things to look for are concessions, certification disputes, litigation over results, and any official or institutional claim that a result does not bind, along with the absence of each.

A note on provenance. Nothing below is `measured`. I did not read a court order, a Truth Social post or a state canvass at first hand for this check-in; every item comes through a press report. Where the report names its primary source (a filing, an order, a post, a recorded statement), the item is `fitted`. Where only one outlet carries it and I could not find a second, it is `assumed`.

## What happened

**Most of the formal process has not happened yet.** Congress Countdown's certification tracker, which says it checks status against official state sources, listed 3 of 50 states as certified on November 16 (South Carolina on November 11, South Dakota and Wyoming on November 10), with four more past their state deadlines but not yet recorded. `fitted`. In Arizona, KTVK/KPHO (azfamily) reports that the Maricopa County board meets November 18 and the statewide canvass is November 23. `fitted`. So this check-in lands before most certification votes. The test is open, not passed.

**Concessions in close and high-profile races.** Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith conceded the Mississippi Senate race to Scott Colom on November 11 in a video posted on X: "While this is not the outcome we worked for or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters," as WLOX quotes it. `fitted`. AP's November 4 roundup (via the Atlanta Journal-Constitution) reports concessions by Representative Max Miller in Ohio and by Republicans Keith Faber and Jay Edwards in Ohio's attorney general and treasurer races. `fitted` for Miller; `assumed` for Faber and Edwards (no statement quoted). The same roundup reports a Democratic candidate in Tennessee, Justin Pearson, saying "I accept the results of this election for Congress" while refusing to accept what he called "rigged maps." `fitted`. That is a challenge to the rules of the contest, not to the count.

**Party leaders.** The Georgia Recorder (Jonathan Shorman, November 11) reports House Speaker Mike Johnson saying the "people have spoken," Senator Ted Cruz calling the results a "Watergate-level repudiation," and Vice President JD Vance attributing the losses to the Iran war without repeating fraud claims. It says nearly all high-ranking elected Republicans have acknowledged the losses. `fitted` for the quotes; `assumed` for the "nearly all" summary.

**The President's position changed.** Al Jazeera (November 9) reports that in the first days after the election Trump congratulated Democrats in a call with CBS News, told Fox News the party "did a good job," and told The New York Times he "absolutely" accepted the results. `fitted`. On November 9 he posted on Truth Social: "Does anybody really believe that this 'Election' was honest," and "How could the Polls be so wrong???" Democracy Docket (Matt Cohen, November 9) adds the line "It's all about the machines, which are far more important than the voter!" Both outlets say he offered no evidence. `fitted`, two outlets quoting the same post. Democracy Docket also reports Trump calling the Alaska Senate result a "disgrace" to reporters on November 16. `assumed`, one outlet.

**The one institutional claim against a result: Alaska.** This is the item that comes closest to the test's terms, and it began before the vote.

- In October the Justice Department sued Alaska asking a federal court to block certification of the Senate race if Senator Dan Sullivan lost by a margin traceable to a second candidate named Dan Sullivan on the ballot. Votebeat (October 29) reports this and that Trump said he personally ordered the suit. `fitted`.
- On November 1, U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed it. CyberScoop quotes the order: "the United States has failed to establish the redressability prerequisite for Article III standing." It reports her reasoning that the requested relief could not work without compromising ballot secrecy and that ballot access is a matter the state controls. `fitted`.
- The Justice Department appealed to the Ninth Circuit. Fox News (November 3) quotes its Rapid Response office: "Alaskan voters deserve a fair election, not to be misled in an attempt to rig outcomes." `fitted`. On November 9 the League of Women Voters of Alaska and Common Cause asked the Ninth Circuit for summary affirmance, per Democracy Docket. `fitted`. No appellate ruling was reported as of this check-in.
- A separate federal criminal investigation into the recruitment of the second candidate continues, per Fox News and Democracy Docket. `fitted` for its existence; nothing public on its scope.
- AP called the race for Mary Peltola on November 17, with Peltola at 50.12 percent in the latest count, so no ranked-choice tabulation was needed, per Alaska's News Source. The race is not yet certified. `fitted`. The Sullivan campaign said on November 16 it would let the process "run its course until every legal vote is counted and certified," as Democracy Docket quotes it, and declined comment after the call. `fitted`. Senator Lisa Murkowski said she would work with Peltola if Peltola is certified, per Democracy Docket. `assumed`, one outlet.

**Other litigation.** In Texas, a Potter County party committee and its chair filed a federal suit on October 27 asking that federal results not be certified in any Texas county until every ballot statewide is hand counted. Democracy Docket's case page shows a motion to dismiss filed November 9, an amended complaint November 14 and a reply November 16, with no ruling. `fitted`. In California, the Justice Department dropped a suit over roughly 20,000 ballots after the Supreme Court rejected its request on the eve of the election, per the Georgia Recorder. `assumed`, one outlet for the outcome. I found no lawsuit by a losing candidate seeking to overturn a 2026 result.

**Candidate-level doubts short of litigation.** In Colorado's 3rd district, the Lauren Boebert campaign's attorney Scott Gessler said: "We are investigating some very unusual voting patterns and expect this election to head to a recount, one way or another." Boebert herself said, "we'll accept whatever outcome is presented." AP called the race for Eileen Laubacher on November 13, 48.8 to 47.9 percent, outside Colorado's automatic recount margin. Democracy Docket reports no concession and no lawsuit as of that date. `fitted`. Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold responded that "Colorado's elections are safe, secure and accurate." `fitted`. Outside office, Mike Lindell called for a "full recount" and Kari Lake wrote that the "math isn't mathing," per the Georgia Recorder. `fitted`. Republican Maricopa County Supervisor Thomas Galvin told fellow Republicans who had been "trafficking in these lies for the last six years, you need to stop." `fitted`.

**Certification refusals.** I found no report of a county or state board refusing to certify a 2026 result. Given how few have certified, that absence means little yet. `assumed` (an absence in what I searched, not a census).

## What it means for the draft's tests

The evidence supports two readings, and this check-in cannot choose between them.

**The reading that acceptance is holding.** Losing candidates in the closest races have conceded or said they will accept the outcome. The Speaker and most senior Republicans have acknowledged the losses. The one institutional attempt to condition a result, the Alaska suit, went through a federal court and lost there on standing and state authority, and the executive's response was an appeal, which is a move inside the same chain of institutions. Recounts that are coming (Arizona's Legislative District 27 is one) are the system's own procedure. In Brinton's terms, none of this is a second chain issuing its own decisions. It is the coordinated chain handling pressure, which is what the draft said a wave election looks like.

**The reading that acceptance is under strain.** The President moved within a week from saying he "absolutely" accepted the results to asking whether the election "was honest." The Justice Department, an arm of the legal government, sought before the vote to have a court withhold certification of a Senate race conditional on its outcome, and is still pursuing that on appeal alongside a criminal probe touching the winning campaign. The losing Alaska incumbent has set certification, not the count, as his point for conceding. That is closer to the draft's thesis 2 than to Brinton's original pattern: not an illegal rival government, but a faction holding part of the legal government and contesting a result from inside it.

**What neither reading shows.** No official or body has claimed that a 2026 result does not bind and then acted on that claim outside a court. No refusal to certify has been reported. Every challenge found so far has been routed through the courts or through rhetoric, and the one court to rule has ruled against it. On the evidence to date, the dual sovereignty test has not been met. It also has not been closed, because most certification votes, the Ninth Circuit appeal and the Texas ruling are still ahead.

**For the other tests.** The Republican statements here (Johnson, Cruz, Galvin, Murkowski, Vance not repeating the fraud claims) are early evidence for the insiders test on [2026-12-04](../willow/watch-list.md), and they cut both ways: acknowledging the loss is not the same as breaking with the administration. The Alaska appeal bears on the check test on 2027-01-04, since it is the same pattern of executive litigation the draft tracks in the district courts, now aimed at a seat in the incoming Senate.

Things worth reading again at the next check-in: state certification counts after the late-November canvass deadlines, any Ninth Circuit action in the Alaska appeal, the Potter County ruling, whether the Sullivan campaign concedes at certification, and whether any certifying body declines to act.
